ηθDNA

Atomistic DNA to the ηθDNA coarse-grained model and back: CG input for AMBER/SIRAH simulations, and all-atom structures from CG frames and trajectories.

This app works on DNA only (with the K⁺/Na⁺ of G-quadruplex channels). To map and backmap a complex with proteins, run the ηθDNA scripts in a terminal (Command line tab), with AMBER and SIRAH. AA = all-atom.

        Then, on your computer

        Hydrogens and a short vacuum minimization need AmberTools. The package runs them on every frame (bash run.sh). For a whole trajectory, the command-line tool does everything at once:

        python backmap_dna.py system.prmtop --traj md.nc -o out/system --n-frames 50

        ηθDNA is a coarse-grained (CG) DNA force field with near-atomic resolution and explicit electrostatics, compatible with the SIRAH force field and the AMBER engine (pmemd.cuda).

        • Backbone. The backbone conformational energy is a 2D correction map (CMAP) fitted to the experimental η–θ map of DNA, mined from 3,862 X-ray structures of naked DNA and protein–DNA complexes at ≤ 2.5 Å.
        • Glycosidic torsion. χ is refitted per base against PDB-mined populations.
        • Base pairing. Specific Lennard-Jones off-diagonal terms for Watson–Crick, Hoogsteen and sugar-edge contacts.

        Together they reproduce canonical and non-canonical motifs: A/B-DNA, Z-DNA, G-quadruplexes, Holliday junctions, hairpins, nucleosomal and circular DNA. Water and ions are SIRAH's (WT4, NaW, KW, ClW), and proteins can be added with SIRAH's protein model.

        The name comes from the two pseudo-torsions the all-atom backbone is contracted to (Duarte & Pyle, 1998), which play the role of φ/ψ for proteins:

        • η: C4′(i−1)–P(i)–C4′(i)–P(i+1)
        • θ: P(i)–C4′(i)–P(i+1)–C4′(i+1)

        Beads

        Each nucleotide keeps its phosphorus, its C4′ and the heavy atoms of the base that carry its pairing and stacking: they keep their positions, and the bead takes the atom's name with the base letter. Charges are those of the CG library (params/dna_vf.lib); every nucleotide carries −1 on its P bead.

        Residue names

        DGX DAX DTX DCXinternal nucleotide
        GX5 AX5 TX5 CX55′ end of a strand (no phosphate)
        GX3 AX3 TX3 CX33′ end of a strand
        DG1 DA1 DT1 DC1"repeat" variant: the 2nd, 4th… of a run of identical internal bases. Its atom types differ, so that AMBER can tell apart the η and θ torsions of the run (params/repeat.lib).
        KW4 NW4K⁺ and Na⁺ in a G-quadruplex channel (bare-ion radii towards the channel beads)

        Force field (ηθDNA 1.0)

        Version 1.0 is the model developed as v18 (v18T29 + 3pB + KW4/NW4). On top of the tleap topology, the build applies, in this order:

        1. CMAP η/θ per base, and CMAP φ/χ per base; the proper torsions on the central P–K and K–N bonds are set to zero.
        2. Junction angles K–N–base, N–K–P and K–P–K at k = 20.
        3. Guanine: P–K–N / N–K–P(i+1) angles and the C4′–N9–N3 / C4′–N9–N7 angles of its glycosidic junction (“kn5”).
        4. C4′ chirality improper P(i)–N–K–P(i+1).
        5. Z typing, from the input geometry: a syn G with a Z backbone, and the C next to it, get the Z values of their junction angles and the Z-specific η/θ grids.
        6. “3pB” torsions base(i)–N(i)–K(i)–P(i+1), except on Z-typed nucleotides.

        The water is SIRAH's WT4, with NaCl 0.15 M on top of neutralization by default; a G-quadruplex always gets channel cations. Production runs carry a flat-bottom restraint on the Watson–Crick H-bonds of the terminal base pairs.

        Backmapping

        The CG beads give the base atoms, C4′ and P of every nucleotide. The rest of the sugar and backbone is placed by internal coordinates (NeRF) from a template. Each nucleotide is built with every template and keeps the one that best fits the beads it does not copy. G-quadruplex channel cations come back as K⁺/Na⁺; water and bulk ions are not reconstructed. In a terminal, backmap_dna.py also rebuilds SIRAH proteins with SIRAH's own recipe (this app skips them).

        This page runs the mapping and backmapping code of the ηθDNA tools in your browser (Python compiled to WebAssembly with Pyodide). Your files are read on your computer and never sent anywhere. The steps that need AmberTools or SIRAH are handed over as small packages to run on your computer.

        In the browserDNA only: AA → CG mapping, G-quadruplex channel cations, heavy-atom backmapping (DNA and channel cations), reading AMBER trajectories
        On your computerthe CG topology (tleap + SIRAH + the ηθDNA parameters), the simulation, the hydrogens and minimization of a backmapped frame (tleap + sander), re-solvation, and everything with proteins: build_dna_protein.py and backmap_dna.py

        Tutorial: from a crystal to a CG simulation and back

        1. In Map, press the 1BNA example, then Map to ηθDNA. The beads appear over the crystal; the strands, the net charge and any issue are listed under it.
        2. Download build package. On a computer with AmberTools and SIRAH, unzip it and run bash 1bna/build.sh: it writes the topology, the AMBER decks and run_md.sh.
        3. Run the simulation with bash 1bna/run_md.sh 1bna (it resumes where it stopped).
        4. In Backmap, choose prmtop + coordinates and give it 1bna.prmtop and md.nc. Pick how many frames, press Backmap, and download the AA PDB, or the package that adds the hydrogens.

        Map: AA → CG

        • Input: a PDB with DNA in ATOM records (DA/DC/DG/DT). Each chain becomes a strand; a TER record also ends one. Modified nucleotides (HETATM, e.g. 5-bromo-dU) are not mapped and break the strand: rename them to the parent base first.
        • G-quadruplexes: tetrads are found by their Hoogsteen pairs; the channel gets one cation per site (between tetrads by default) unless the input already has K⁺/Na⁺ in it.
        • Protein–DNA complexes: the app works on DNA only, and only the DNA of a complex would be mapped. Build them with build_dna_protein.py in a terminal (pdb2pqr protonation and SIRAH's cgconv.pl for the protein).

        Backmap: CG → AA

        • CG PDB: from the Map tab, from convert_dna.py, or frames written by cpptraj (one MODEL per frame). The links between residues are found as tleap makes them, within each TER segment.
        • prmtop + coordinates: the CG topology of the run and an AMBER NetCDF trajectory (md.nc), a restart (.rst7/.inpcrd) or a PDB in the same atom order. This is the exact route of backmap_dna.py.
        • Output: one PDB with the heavy atoms of every frame (DNA and channel cations; SIRAH protein residues are skipped, backmap_dna.py rebuilds them), and the template each nucleotide took. The hydrogenation package adds hydrogens and runs the same 150-step vacuum minimization as backmap_dna.py, then writes one prmtop and one trajectory.

        Command line

        The tools, the ηθDNA 1.0 parameters and every option of build_dna.py, build_dna_protein.py, backmap_dna.py and run_md.sh are in the Command line tab.

        Limits of the web app

        • Up to 100 frames per backmap run, and files that fit in the browser's memory (the trajectory is read frame by frame).
        • DNA only: complexes with proteins are mapped and backmapped with the scripts, in a terminal.
        • Backmapped frames have heavy atoms only, unminimized: they are a starting point for the hydrogenation package or for your own minimization.
